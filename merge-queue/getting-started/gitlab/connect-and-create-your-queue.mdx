Trunk acts on GitLab through a **service account** in one of your top-level groups. The service account has the **Maintainer** role on the group, so Trunk can open merge requests, push test branches, read pipelines and merge in every project the group contains, including subgroups and projects added later.

## Prerequisites

- The **admin** role in your Trunk organization
- The **Owner** role on the GitLab top-level group that contains your project (for Option A), or a service account that a group Owner created for you (for Option B)
- Your project is on GitLab.com. Self-managed GitLab instances are not supported yet.

## Connect GitLab

1. [Sign in to app.trunk.io](https://app.trunk.io/login) and go to **Settings** → **Integrations** → **GitLab**.
2. Click **Connect GitLab** (or **Add Connection** if your organization already has one).
3. Choose one of the two options below.

### Option A: Connect with GitLab (recommended)

Trunk creates and manages the service account for you. This option requires the **Owner** role on the top-level group.

1. Click **Connect with GitLab** and authorize Trunk on GitLab.com. Trunk asks for the `api` scope and uses your authorization only once, to set up its own service account. It then discards your authorization.
2. Under **Choose a group**, select the top-level group that contains your project. Subgroups are not listed: Trunk connects a whole top-level group.
3. Click **Create service account & connect**.

Trunk then:

- Creates a group service account named **Trunk Merge Queue** (GitLab chooses its username).
- Gives it the **Maintainer** role on the group.
- Issues its own access tokens for the account, with the `api` scope.

<Note>
  If you see "You need the Owner role on this group to create a service account", ask a group Owner to complete these steps, or use Option B with a service account they create.
</Note>

### Option B: Create the service account yourself

Use this option if your GitLab policies require you to create and manage the account.

1. In GitLab, go to your top-level group → **Settings** → **Service accounts**.
2. Create a service account, for example `Trunk Merge Queue`.
3. Create a personal access token for the service account with the `api` scope.
4. Add the service account as a **Maintainer** of the group, or of each project you want Trunk to manage.
5. In Trunk, paste the token into **Personal Access Token** and click **Validate & Continue**.
6. Check the account, token scopes, memberships and accessible projects that Trunk shows, then click **Confirm & Save**. If the token can reach more than one top-level group, pick the group to connect.

<Warning>
  **Trunk cannot replace a token that you paste in.** When the token expires, the queue stops merging until the connection is fixed. Set an expiration date that matches your rotation policy, and [contact support](/setup-and-administration/support) before it expires. Option A does not have this limit.
</Warning>

<Note>
  You can paste a personal access token that belongs to a person instead of a service account. Trunk accepts it with a warning: the queue then acts as that person, and stops working if they lose access or leave.
</Note>

## Create your first queue

1. In the **Merge Queue** tab, click **New Queue**.
2. Select your GitLab project from the dropdown and enter the target branch to merge into (for example, `main`).
3. Click **Create Queue**.

When you create the queue, Trunk adds a **project webhook** named `Trunk` to the GitLab project. It delivers push, merge request, comment, pipeline and job events to Trunk. You don't need to add a webhook yourself.

<Note>
  **Don't edit or delete the `Trunk` webhook.** Trunk signs its webhook deliveries and rejects hooks that use only a secret token. If GitLab disables the hook after repeated failed deliveries, re-enable it in the project's **Settings** → **Webhooks**. Trunk does not detect a disabled hook, so queued merge requests stop moving until you do.
</Note>

## What you just did

You connected a GitLab group to Trunk and created a queue for one project's target branch. Trunk can now see the project, but your queue won't merge anything until the project settings in the next step are in place.

<Info>
  **Need multiple queues?** You can create more queues for the same project that target other branches (for example, `release/v2`). See [Multiple queues per repository](/merge-queue/administration/advanced-settings#multiple-queues-per-repository).
</Info>
