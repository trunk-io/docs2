Trunk acts on GitLab through a **service account** in one of your top-level groups. The service account has the **Maintainer** role on the group, so Trunk can open merge requests, push test branches, read pipelines and merge in every project the group contains, including subgroups and projects added later.

## Prerequisites

- The **admin** role in your Trunk organization
- The **Owner** role on the GitLab top-level group that contains your project
- Your project is on GitLab.com. Self-managed GitLab instances are not supported yet.

## Connect GitLab

Trunk creates the service account and its tokens for you.

1. [Sign in to app.trunk.io](https://app.trunk.io/login) and go to **Settings** → **Integrations** → **GitLab**.
2. Click **Connect GitLab** (or **Add Connection** if your organization already has one).
3. Click **Connect with GitLab** and authorize Trunk on GitLab.com. Trunk asks for the `api` scope and uses your authorization only once, to set up its own service account. It then discards your authorization.
4. Under **Choose a group**, select the top-level group that contains your project. Only top-level groups that you own are listed: Trunk connects a whole top-level group.
5. Click **Create service account & connect**.

Trunk then:

- Creates a group service account named **Trunk Merge Queue** (GitLab chooses its username).
- Gives it the **Maintainer** role on the group.
- Issues its own access tokens for the account, with the `api` scope.

<Note>
  If you see "You are not an Owner of any top-level GitLab group" or "GitLab refused the request. You need the Owner role on this group to create a service account", ask a group Owner to complete these steps. They also need the **admin** role in Trunk: invite them in **Settings** → **Organization** → **Team**.
</Note>

## Create your first queue

1. Open the **Merge Queue** tab. If your organization has no queues yet, it opens the setup form. Otherwise, open the actions menu and choose **Create queue**.
2. Under **Select a repository**, pick your GitLab project. Enter the target branch to merge into (for example, `main`) in **Branch Name**.
3. Click **Create Queue**.

If GitLab isn't connected yet, the **Merge Queue** tab shows **Connect GitLab**, which opens the setup above.

When you create the queue, Trunk adds a **project webhook** named `Trunk` to the GitLab project. It delivers push, merge request, comment, pipeline and job events to Trunk. You don't need to add a webhook yourself.

<Note>
  **Don't edit or delete the `Trunk` webhook.** Trunk signs its deliveries, so a hook that you add or edit by hand doesn't work. If GitLab disables the hook after repeated failed deliveries, re-enable it in the project's **Settings** → **Webhooks**. Trunk doesn't detect a disabled or missing hook, so queued merge requests stop moving until you fix it. If the hook is missing, [contact support](/setup-and-administration/support).
</Note>

## What you just did

You connected a GitLab group to Trunk and created a queue for one project's target branch. Trunk can now see the project, but your queue won't merge anything until the project settings in the next step are in place.

<Info>
  **Need multiple queues?** You can create more queues for the same project that target other branches (for example, `release/v2`). See [Multiple queues per repository](/merge-queue/administration/advanced-settings#multiple-queues-per-repository).
</Info>

## Disconnecting GitLab

To disconnect, open the connection in **Settings** → **Integrations** → **GitLab** and delete it. You can't disconnect while any of the group's projects has a merge queue: delete those queues first. Trunk revokes its tokens. It doesn't delete the service account: remove it from the group in GitLab yourself.
