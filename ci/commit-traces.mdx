A commit trace is the whole picture of what CI did on one commit: every workflow
that ran, every re-run of each, every job inside them, and the time each job
spent queued before a runner picked it up.

It is the same view in two places — the **CI** tab of a
[pull request](/ci/pull-requests), and the CI tab's job history. Clicking a run
anywhere in Trunk lands here.

## Reading it

Each row is a lane. Workflows are the top level, jobs nest inside them, and
steps nest inside jobs. A bar's position and width are when the work happened
and how long it took.

- **Diagonal stripes are queue time** — the job was waiting for a runner, not
  doing work. Nothing else in the view is striped.
- **Colour is outcome.** A running job is drawn as running, never as the grey a
  skipped one gets.
- **The axis measures elapsed active time.** A re-run hours after the original
  would otherwise push everything real into a sliver, so idle stretches are
  compressed into a labelled break ("2h later") and each side keeps its own
  scale.

Click any bar to open its details: timings, outcome, the attributes Trunk
recorded, and — where logs are available — the job's log output.

## Moving around

- **Just start typing** to filter. There is no search box to click into; the
  first printable key opens one. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to commit the query,
  then <kbd>Enter</kbd> and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd> to step through
  matches. <kbd>Esc</kbd> clears it.
- **+ steps** expands every job on screen down to its steps. Steps are collapsed
  by default — a run has several times more steps than jobs, and the full tree
  buries the shape. A single job's chevron expands just that one.
- **+ N skipped jobs** reveals jobs that did not run. They are hidden by default,
  and hiding them also takes their time off the axis — so a matrix guard that
  fired ten minutes after the real work does not stretch the chart to nothing.

Every attempt of every workflow is on screen at once, each on its own row. There
is no attempt selector, so a link to a specific job always resolves.

## Other checks

A lane named **Other checks** holds checks posted through the GitHub Checks API
rather than dispatched as jobs of a workflow.

GitHub reports these as jobs of whichever workflow happened to open the first
check suite on the commit, which is usually unrelated to the one that created
them — so Trunk lifts them out rather than showing them as work that workflow
performed. They are real CI signal on the commit; they just have no workflow
run, and no queue time, of their own.

See [GitHub Actions](/ci/get-started/github-actions) for why GitHub reports them
this way.
